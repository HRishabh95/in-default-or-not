### A.2 A filed indenture where a bankruptcy filing does not cause automatic acceleration

**Valley National Bancorp, 6.25% Fixed-to-Floating Rate Subordinated Notes due 2032.** Second Supplemental Indenture dated September 20, 2022, to the Indenture dated May 28, 2021 (U.S. Bank Trust Company, N.A., trustee). 8-K EX-4.2:
https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/714310/000119312522247735/d405024dex42.htm

- A voluntary filing is an Event of Default. §5.1(b): "the Company or a Principal Subsidiary Bank shall commence a voluntary case under any applicable bankruptcy, insolvency or other similar law now or hereafter in effect …"
- Acceleration requires a declaration. §5.2: "If an Event of Default under clause (a) or (b) of Section 5.1 hereof with respect to the Notes occurs and is continuing, then the Trustee or the Holders of not less than 25% in aggregate principal amount of the Outstanding Notes may declare the principal of all the Notes to be due and payable immediately, by a notice in writing to the Company (and to the Trustee if given by the Holders), and upon any such declaration such principal or such lesser amount shall become immediately due and payable."
- The indenture has no automatic-acceleration clause. I searched it for "automatic". The cover 8-K says: "Payment of principal on the Notes may be accelerated in the case of certain events of bankruptcy or insolvency involving the Company or the Bank. There is no automatic acceleration or right of acceleration in the case of default in the payment of interest" (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/714310/000119312522247735/d405024d8k.htm).

This is one verified example: a bank-holding-company subordinated note. I did not establish how common this structure is.

### A.3 Does the automatic stay cure the default, or restrain enforcement?

The automatic stay restrains enforcement and collection. It does not cure or suspend the event of default. Under 11 U.S.C. §362(a), a petition "operates as a stay, applicable to all entities, of— (1) the commencement or continuation … of a judicial, administrative, or other action or proceeding against the debtor … (6) any act to collect, assess, or recover a claim against the debtor that arose before the commencement of the case." Section 362(d) lets the court grant "relief from the stay … such as by terminating, annulling, modifying, or conditioning such stay." (Source: LII, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/362.) The US Courts page "Chapter 11 – Bankruptcy Basics" says the same thing: "The automatic stay provides a period of time in which all judgments, collection activities, foreclosures, and repossessions of property are suspended and may not be pursued by the creditors on any debt or claim that arose before the filing of the bankruptcy petition" (https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-11-bankruptcy-basics).

Neither text says anything about curing or suspending a contractual default. Both describe a restraint on acts and proceedings. This is also how Chesapeake, Intelsat and Whiting described it in their 8-Ks: the filing "constitutes an event of default that accelerated" the debt, and enforcement "efforts … are automatically stayed". Scope: I read §362 and the US Courts page only. I did not review other Bankruptcy Code provisions, such as §365(e), §541(c) or plan-level cure and reinstatement.

## B. Does Item C.9 apply to loans?

### B.1 Form N-PORT text

Source: https://www.sec.gov/files/formn-port.pdf. This is the reference copy "effective December 11, 2023"; the PDF was last modified 2024-12-03.

- Item C.4: "**Item C.4. Asset and issuer type.** Select the category that most closely identifies the instrument among each of the following: a. Asset type (short-term investment vehicle (e.g., money market fund, liquidity pool, or other cash management vehicle), repurchase agreement, equity-common, equity-preferred, debt, derivative-commodity, derivative-credit, derivative-equity, derivative-foreign exchange, derivative-interest rate, derivatives-other, structured note, loan, ABS-mortgage backed security, ABS-asset backed commercial paper, ABS-collateralized bond/debt obligation, ABS-other, commodity, real estate, other)."
- Item C.9: "**Item C.9. For debt securities, also provide:** a. Maturity date. b. Coupon. … c. Currently in default? [Y/N] d. Are there any interest payments in arrears or have any coupon payments been legally deferred by the issuer? [Y/N] e. Is any portion of the interest paid in kind? [Y/N] …"
- "Debt security" is not defined. General Instruction E (Definitions) defines Class, Exchange-Traded Fund, Fund, ISIN, LEI, Multiple Class Fund, Registrant, Series, Swap and VaR Ratio, but not "debt security". The form does not say whether asset type "loan" falls under Item C.9.
- General Instruction G: "A Fund is not required to respond to an item that is wholly inapplicable (for example, no response would be required for Item C.11 when reporting information about an investment that is not a derivative)." The same instruction lets funds use "their own internal methodologies and the conventions of their service providers," provided they are "consistently applied".

### B.3 Technical specification and XML schema

Source: EDGAR Form N-PORT XML Technical Specification v1.13 (March 2025), https://www.sec.gov/files/edgar/filer-information/specifications/edgar-form-n-port-xml-tech-spec-113.zip (PDF plus XSDs).

- `debtSec` is optional and is not conditioned on `assetCat`. The element table lists "5 22 NV debtSec … o o o o 1", where "o" means "Optional." for every submission type. Its children, including `isDefault`, are rule "m#26": "If <maturityDt> is provided, then the element or attribute becomes mandatory". No rule ties `debtSec` to any asset category.
- In `eis_NPORT_Filer.xsd`, `DEBT_SECURITY_GROUP` is annotated "These elements are part of Item C.9. For debt securities". It is referenced inside `invstOrSec` as `<xs:group ref="DEBT_SECURITY_GROUP" minOccurs="0" maxOccurs="1" />`. The schema neither permits nor forbids it by asset category, so `debtSec` is schema-valid for `assetCat` = LON. The spec's code table lists "LON Loan" and "DBT Debt" as separate codes.
- The spec also warns: "EDGAR performs several validation checks that are not related to the schema files." Table 8-1 lists some of those business rules, and none concerns asset category versus `debtSec`. Table 8-1 is "not an all-inclusive list", so I cannot rule out an unpublished server-side rule.

### B.5 Adopting release IC-32314 (Oct. 13, 2016) and later releases

Source: https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2016/33-10231.pdf (Release Nos. 33-10231; 34-79095; IC-32314).

- Section "ii. Debt Securities": "Form N-PORT would require additional information about each debt security held by the fund in order to gain transparency into the payment flows and potential convertibility into equity of such investments".
- On the default flag, a commenter argued these items "require a subjective legal analysis on an instrument-by-instrument basis, on which conclusions among funds may vary". The Commission replied: "Rather than provide funds with a definition that may not be applicable in all situations, or inconsistent with their financial statement reporting, we believe that it is more appropriate to allow funds to continue to use their own methodology in responding to these items on Form N-PORT, subject to the limitations of General Instruction G."
- The release does not say whether loans fall under C.9. In the C.4 discussion it lists "loan" as an asset type without addressing C.9. A commenter it quotes refers to "syndicated loans" in the context of risk metrics. A Commission statement also refers to "default or arrears relating to a fund's investments in private issuer debt".
- Later releases: IC-35308 (Aug. 2024; https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2024/ic-35308.pdf) has no amendment to N-PORT Item C.9. The "Item C.9" in that release is Form N-CEN's "Investment advisers" item. I did not open other releases, the EDGAR Filer Manual, or staff no-action letters.

**Answer:** The form, FAQ, schema and adopting release I opened neither include nor exclude loans from Item C.9. The schema allows `debtSec` on LON holdings. At least one large filer populates it for every loan. The Commission explicitly left the definition of "default" to each fund's own methodology.

## Summary: what the note can and cannot state

1. **Can state:** In all five cases, every governing document I opened makes the issuer's own voluntary bankruptcy filing an Event of Default with automatic acceleration. That is 5 note indentures, 4 credit agreements and 1 revolver. Endo's notes accelerate automatically only for a filing by the Parent, which did file. The issuers' 8-Ks say the same. **Cannot state:** that this holds for every tranche, or for the exact tranches our funds held.
2. **Can state:** Automatic acceleration is not universal. A filed counterexample is Valley National's 2032 subordinated notes, which need a declaration by the Trustee or 25% of holders. **Cannot state:** how common that structure is.
3. **Can state:** §362 and US Courts describe the stay as suspending collection and enforcement, not as curing the default. A holder that reports "N" after a petition therefore cannot rely on the stay as the reason. **Cannot state:** anything from Code sections I did not read.
4. **Can state:** The SEC has given no form instruction, FAQ or schema rule on whether loans (asset type LON) belong in Item C.9. The schema allows it, filers do it, and the Commission left "default" to fund methodology. **Cannot state:** that loan flags are required, or that they are comparable across funds.
