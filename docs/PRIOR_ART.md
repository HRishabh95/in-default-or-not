# Prior-art review: N-PORT "currently in default" flag after bankruptcy

Reviewed 2026-10-29. Bounded review: each source below was opened and read unless marked "no". Content of sources I could not open is not inferred. Quotes are verbatim from the opened source.

Claim under review: in 57 verified US bankruptcy cases (2019-2026), about 25% of registered-fund holdings of debtor-issued prepetition bonds and loans were reported "not in default" at the first post-petition report; funds holding the same security on the same date disagree; disagreement persists and clusters by fund family; prices show no systematic gap between flaggers and non-flaggers; staff comment letters show isolated omitted flags.

## Two facts from the form itself (correct before drafting)

- **Item number.** "Currently in default" is **Item C.9.c**, not C.9.b.ii. In Form N-PORT, C.9.b.ii is "Annualized rate". The form reads: "c. Currently in default? [Y/N]" and "d. Are there any interest payments in arrears or have any coupon payments been legally deferred by the issuer? [Y/N]" (https://www.sec.gov/files/formn-port.pdf). The SEC data-set readme maps `IS_DEFAULT` to C.9.c, `ANNUALIZED_RATE` to C.9.b.ii and `ARE_ANY_INTEREST_PAYMENT` to C.9.d (https://sec.gov/files/nport_readme.pdf).
- **No definition.** Form N-PORT does not define "default". In the form text, the word appears only in the C.9.c question and in "credit default swaps". The note must therefore frame "not in default" as an inconsistency between funds, not as a misreport against a defined rule. The one exception: a bankruptcy filing is commonly an event of default, and Bloomberg's own definition treats it as one (see row 3).

## Verdict (5 lines)

1. **Not new:** a commercial security-level N-PORT default flag (ICE sells "Debt default flag"/"Interest in arrears", and Bloomberg has "Is Defaulted"). Also not new: comparing the same security across funds on the same date via N-PORT (Morningstar 2021; Cici-Gibson-Merrick 2011 pre-N-PORT), and the finding that individual funds omit C.9.c flags (SEC staff letters: Goldman 2020, RiverNorth 2021, plus OFS Credit 2021 and Yieldstreet 2025, which the note omits).
2. **Plausibly new (no prior study found, in a bounded search):** a systematic, bankruptcy-anchored measure of how often registered funds report debtor prepetition debt as "not in default" (about 25%), with same-security, same-date disagreement, persistence and fund-family clustering of the filed flag.
3. **Only an add-on to known work:** "no systematic price gap between flaggers and non-flaggers". Cite Morningstar as the price-dispersion baseline and do not frame this as a pricing discovery.
4. **Must fix or hedge:** the item is C.9.c, not C.9.b.ii. The form does not define "default", so call the result "inconsistent reporting" rather than "misreporting". Family clustering may reflect shared vendor reference data (ICE-type flags), not fund judgment. Change "isolated cases" in the staff-letter sentence to "recurring staff comments" unless the remaining 31 search hits are read.
5. **Not cleared:** Empirasign (403), the Morningstar companion article (403), Manoel 2026 (403, the closest population) and the other vendors' (LSEG, S&P, Moody's, FactSet) field documentation were not opened. Check these manually before claiming "first".
